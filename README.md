# Employee Data Summary

**Task 31 – Employee Data Summary**  
Veda Technology | Data Analytics Track

## About this task

For this task, I used the provided employee dataset in Excel to summarize the workforce by department, job role and salary. I organized the information into summary tables and created three charts to make the results easier to understand.

## Dataset

- Total employee records: **243**
- Departments: **6**
- Job roles: **19**
- Columns: EmployeeID, Department, JobRole, Age, YearsAtCompany and MonthlySalary
- Missing values: **None** in the supplied dataset

## Summary and key findings

- The department with the most employees is **Sales**, with **56 employees**.
- The highest average monthly salary by department is in **Finance** (approximately **6,011.29**).
- The lowest average monthly salary by department is in **HR** (approximately **4,348.00**).
- Overall average monthly salary: **5,170.37**.
- Overall median monthly salary: **4,350.00**.

## Workbook sheets

- **Summary:** key metrics, department summary and three charts.
- **Employee Data:** the supplied employee-level data.
- **Department Summary:** employee count, average salary, median salary and total monthly salary by department.
- **Role Summary:** employee count and average and median monthly salary by department and job role.

## Charts included

1. Employees by Department
2. Average Monthly Salary by Department
3. Average Monthly Salary by Job Role

## Why use median salary?

The median is the middle salary after sorting the values. It is useful alongside the average because a few unusually high salaries can pull the average upward. Looking at both gives a more balanced picture.

## What I learned

- How to group employee data by department and job role.
- How to calculate employee counts and salary averages and medians.
- How to compare departments using summary tables and charts.
- Why the median can provide useful context alongside the average.
